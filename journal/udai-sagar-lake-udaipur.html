Udai Sagar Lake: Udaipur's quiet, wide-open lake

Most visitors to Udaipur never make it to Udai Sagar, and that's exactly why it's special. About 13 km east of the old city, this broad lake is ringed by the Aravalli hills, dotted with fishing boats and blissfully free of traffic.

A lake older than most of the city

Udai Sagar was commissioned by Maharana Udai Singh II, the founder of Udaipur, and completed in the 1560s by damming the Berach river. It was built to secure water for his new capital, and it is one of the oldest of Udaipur's lakes. It's roughly 4 km long and fills beautifully after the monsoon.

What to do at Udai Sagar

  • Watch the sunrise from the shore, when the water is glassy and the hills turn pink
  • Birdwatching: herons, egrets, cormorants and kingfishers are regulars, with migratory birds in winter
  • Photography at golden hour, when the palms and hills reflect in the lake
  • A slow evening by a bonfire, with nothing but stars and the sound of water

Best time to visit

October to March is ideal, with cool mornings, clear skies and full water after the rains. July to September brings green hills and dramatic monsoon clouds. April to June is hot, but the lake breeze and a pool make it pleasant, and it's the best value time to stay.

Staying on the lake

The Udaisarovar sits right on the shore at Lakadwas. It has four wooden cottages named Air, Earth, Water and Fire, plus a family suite, a lakefront infinity pool, horse riding and home-cooked Mewari food. The city's sights are about a 30-minute drive away, and we can arrange the car.
