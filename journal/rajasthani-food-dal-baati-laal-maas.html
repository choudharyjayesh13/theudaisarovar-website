Dal baati, laal maas & the taste of Mewar

Rajasthani food was shaped by the desert. Ingredients had to keep, water was scarce, and ghee, millet, pulses and dried berries did the heavy lifting. The result is some of India's most comforting and distinctive cooking. Here's what to try in Udaipur, and what our kitchen at The Udaisarovar cooks every day.

Dal Baati Churma

The state's signature meal. Baatis are hard wheat rolls baked until crisp, then cracked open and soaked in ghee. They come with panchmel dal, made from five lentils, and churma, crushed baati sweetened with jaggery or sugar. Eat it with your hands.

Laal Maas

Mewar's legendary red mutton curry. It was once a hunters' dish, slow-cooked with red Mathania chillies, garlic, curd and whole spices until the gravy turns deep red. It's fiery, rich and unforgettable. At The Udaisarovar we cook it fresh on advance order.

Gatte ki Sabzi

Gram-flour dough is rolled, boiled and cut into dumplings, then simmered in a tangy yoghurt gravy. It was invented for days when fresh vegetables were hard to find.

Ker Sangri

Ker are desert berries and sangri are beans. Dried and then tempered with chilli and amchur, they are the true taste of the Thar.

Bajre ki Roti, Lahsun Chutney & Kadhi

Millet rotis with fiery garlic chutney and a dollop of white butter, best in winter. Rajasthani kadhi, light and tangy, is the perfect partner with rice.

Udaipur breakfasts

Poha, pyaaz kachori, mirchi vada and strong masala chai. Have them on the shore while the mist lifts off the lake.

Homegrown, home-style

Our kitchen uses seasonal vegetables and herbs from our garden and nearby farms, and cooks everything fresh to order. Want it less spicy? Just tell the chef.
