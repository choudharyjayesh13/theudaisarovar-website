Udaipur, the City of Lakes

Few cities in India are as tied to water as Udaipur. Founded in 1559 by Maharana Udai Singh II in a ring of Aravalli hills, the city grew around a chain of man-made lakes. Each one was built by a Mewar ruler to store monsoon rain, feed farms and cool the city. Today they give Udaipur its nickname, its sunsets and its slow pace.

Lake Pichola: the heart of the old city

The oldest and most famous lake, created in the 14th century, is home to the Lake Palace and Jag Mandir, with the City Palace rising from its eastern shore. Take a boat from the City Palace jetty in the late afternoon, then watch the sun go down from Ambrai Ghat.

Fateh Sagar: the city's favourite evening hangout

First built in the late 1600s and later rebuilt by Maharana Fateh Singh, Fateh Sagar is where Udaipur comes to breathe. Locals walk the pal (embankment), boats cross to Nehru Garden, and stalls sell roasted corn and coffee. Moti Magri, with its Maharana Pratap memorial, overlooks the lake.

Udai Sagar: the quiet one

East of the city, built by Udaipur's founder himself, Udai Sagar Lake is wide, calm and framed by hills. There are no crowds and no traffic, just herons, fishing boats and huge skies. It's where The Udaisarovar sits, right on the shore.

Badi Lake & Jaisamand

Badi Lake, to the north-west, was built by Maharana Raj Singh I and is a lovely sunrise drive. Further away, Jaisamand (Dhebar), built in the late 1600s, is one of the largest artificial lakes in Asia, and a great half-day trip.

How to see the lakes

  • Morning: sunrise at Udai Sagar or Badi Lake, when the light is soft and the water still
  • Afternoon: City Palace and a Pichola boat ride
  • Sunset: Fateh Sagar, Ambrai Ghat or Sajjangarh (Monsoon Palace), with views over several lakes at once
